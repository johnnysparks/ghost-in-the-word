# Ghost in the Word — project plan

Living specification · October 14, 2026 · Corpus/alignment pipeline, embedding experiments, and the first atlas UI are implemented; evaluation remains pending.

Build an interactive translation comparison tool that helps a reader find where Bible translations diverge, inspect the language behind those differences, and eventually investigate their relationship to source texts.

Provisional starting scope: English-to-English comparisons, three traceable editions, a book-level pilot, then broader coverage. Cross-language and source-language comparisons remain explicit research milestones. These are recommendations, not settled user preferences.

## 1. What the vectors can tell us

The motivating hypothesis is that different translations are observations of a shared underlying meaning. That is a useful research direction. A text embedding, however, is a model's representation of a passage, not an observed language-independent ground truth.

Keep three questions separate:

| Question | Proposed measurement | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How differently do A and B render this passage? | Distance between their embeddings; separate wording difference | Model-estimated divergence; does not identify which translation is better |
| How unusual is a rendering among other translations? | Distance from an explicitly chosen reference panel | Agreement with that panel; shared ancestry and selection affect the result |
| How well does a rendering preserve a source passage? | Source-conditioned assessment calibrated against expert judgments | A later, evaluated faithfulness heuristic for a specified source edition |

For normalized embeddings generated by the same model and preprocessing recipe:

`distance(A, B, passage) = 1 - dot(embedding(A), embedding(B))`

Cosine distance has a theoretical range of 0–2. Its practical scale depends on the model and text population. Do not turn it into a percentage of faithfulness. A percentile can say how unusual a distance is within a named translation pair and corpus scope; it is neither a probability nor comparable across arbitrary reference populations.

Meaning differences can coexist with very similar vocabulary. Conversely, modernization, idiom, or word order can change the wording while preserving meaning. Differences can also arise from the source edition, surrounding context, a missing passage, or an alignment error. The product must make those possibilities inspectable.

### Preserving the latent-vector idea

Treat a per-passage latent representation as an estimated reference, conditional on a model, source edition, and reference panel. Begin research with a weighted mean of normalized reference embeddings, normalized again, plus a dispersion measure. Compare this with a medoid: an actual reference rendering closest to the others.

- Exclude the two translations under comparison from their reference panel. With only two translations, their midpoint makes both equally distant by construction and supplies no evidence about faithfulness.
- Give related translation families limited combined weight; duplicating closely related revisions should not move the estimated reference substantially.
- Repeat the estimate across panel selections and models. Label the variation as sensitivity, not calibrated confidence.
- If reference translations form distinct interpretive clusters, preserve those clusters instead of presenting their average as the single correct meaning.
- Source anchoring and expert labels are necessary to investigate fidelity; a consensus estimator alone cannot identify original intent.
- Do not train the model simply to collapse all versions of a verse together. That objective could erase the very translation differences the product is meant to surface.

## 2. The interaction to build

The main workflow is: choose two editions → choose a book or passage → find an interesting difference → read both renderings in context → inspect the evidence and share the selection.

### Primary view: divergence across a book

- Translation A and B selectors, book/chapter selector, reference search, and verse/passage granularity.
- Horizontal axis: aligned passage order. Vertical axis: model-estimated semantic distance.
- Hover or keyboard focus previews the reference, both texts, and the score. Click selects the passage and synchronizes every view.
- Brush a range to zoom to a chapter or passage. Offer canonical order and a ranked list of the largest differences.
- At whole-Bible scale, show book/chapter summaries with distributions and counts. Drill into verse points; do not squeeze all passages into an unreadable line.
- Unmapped, missing, and uncertain units appear as distinct statuses, never distance zero. Do not connect a line across a gap.

### Secondary view: wording versus semantic distance

One point per aligned unit. Horizontal axis: normalized wording difference. Vertical axis: embedding distance. This makes passages with very different wording but low model-estimated semantic distance easy to inspect, alongside passages with little wording change but higher semantic distance.

These regions are investigative leads, not automatic classifications. Wording distance initially applies only to same-language comparisons. Hide or replace that axis for cross-language comparisons rather than comparing unrelated scripts with an English token diff.

### Linked reader

Show both full renderings, previous/next context, and optional same-language word-diff highlighting. Offer footnotes separately from main text. Show edition provenance, alignment status, score method, and a plain-language explanation of what the metric measures in an expandable details panel.

Word highlighting describes textual edits; it is not an attribution of the embedding score. Automated explanations, if added later, must cite the displayed text, separate observation from interpretation, and remain independently labeled model output.

Use a shareable URL containing edition IDs, passage, model run, and chart settings. Provide a keyboard-accessible results table, readable labels, and non-color status indicators. On narrow screens, stack the chart and reader.

### Later exploration

For a selected passage, show the distance matrix across several translations. A two-dimensional embedding map can be an optional exploration aid, with distances calculated in the original space. Projected spacing is approximate; the axes do not mean literalness, faithfulness, or theology.

Defer a universal translation leaderboard, a single faithfulness grade, accounts, annotation collaboration, and automatic theological judgments.

## 3. Corpus recommendation

Prefer edition-specific publisher or custodian downloads with explicit provenance and usage terms.

| Source | Proposed role | Findings |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [World English Bible at eBible](https://ebible.org/find/details.php?id=engwebp) | Initial modern-English edition | Public-domain text with structured downloads, including USFM; pin the specific `engwebp` edition and snapshot |
| [Berean Standard publisher downloads](https://berean.bible/downloads.htm) | Initial comparison edition | Text, USFM, USJ, and USX downloads; publisher [states the text is public domain](https://berean.bible/licensing.htm) |
| [ASV 1901 at eBible](https://ebible.org/find/details.php?id=eng-asv) | Third edition and older-language contrast | Catalog identifies the edition and public-domain status; inspect the chosen download during ingestion |
| [thiagobodruk/bible](https://github.com/thiagobodruk/bible) | Useful adapter/reference source | Convenient JSON/XML catalog, but documents encoding issues, coverage gaps, and verse-structure differences; its license statement also reserves owners' rights |
| [eBible catalog](https://ebible.org/Scriptures/) | Additional languages and editions | Discover editions here, checking each edition's terms and completeness individually |

The WEB text is public domain while its name is a trademark; retain the unaltered display text and keep analysis normalization separate. See its [publisher notice](https://ebible.org/engwebp/copyright.htm). Availability in an aggregate repository is not sufficient evidence of rights to redistribute every included translation.

Start with WEB versus BSB as the default pair and ASV as an alternative. This is a convenient pilot, not a representative consensus panel; relationships between editions must be documented before any consensus experiment.

For later source-language work, investigate [Macula Hebrew](https://github.com/Clear-Bible/macula-hebrew), which combines Westminster Leningrad Codex text with separately sourced linguistic annotations, and the [CNTR Statistical Restoration Greek New Testament](https://github.com/Center-for-New-Testament-Restoration/SR), available under CC BY 4.0. These are candidate source editions, not a uniquely authoritative original. Verify each text/annotation license separately and support additional textual traditions before broad fidelity claims.

## 4. Alignment is part of the model

Never assume that identical chapter/verse numbers or array offsets guarantee equivalent content. Use explicit source references and a versioned mapping into internal comparison units.

Support one-to-one, one-to-many, many-to-one, missing, and unresolved mappings. Store verse ranges and subverse labels. A merged rendering should be compared with the equivalent combined span, not duplicated against every individual verse. Distinguish absence in an edition from incomplete ingestion.

Validate book identities, canon membership, chapter/verse numbering, duplicate references, empty strings, Unicode, superscriptions, notes, and verse bridges. Preserve different canons and versification systems in metadata even if the first release covers only the shared 66-book collection.

A source-text variant is a documented textual difference, not automatically a translation error. Do not infer a manuscript cause from distance alone.

Suggested records:

- `Edition`: ID, language/script, full edition name, revision, canon, versification, source URL, terms, attribution, source-tradition and family metadata where known.
- `TextSegment`: edition, original reference/range, display text, analysis text, notes, source snapshot and hash.
- `AlignmentUnit`: stable internal ID, mapped segment IDs, mapping method/version, status, review evidence.
- `EmbeddingRun`: exact model revision, preprocessing version, input hashes, context strategy, dimensions, truncation flags, compute metadata.
- `Comparison`: alignment unit, pair, embedding run, raw distance, wording metric, optional percentile with explicit reference population.
- `Assessment`: reviewer/model origin, judgment type, evidence, uncertainty or disagreement, and version.

Embed the text without translation names or book/chapter/verse identifiers: those shared labels could make aligned verses look artificially similar. Preserve punctuation, negation, names, and meaningful distinctions. Do not silently modernize spelling. Record preprocessing choices so their effects can be evaluated.

Compute verse-only scores first. Evaluate equivalent multi-verse windows separately, because shared neighboring text can dilute a changed phrase. Display passage scores as passage scores; do not silently substitute them for verse scores.

## 5. Validate the signal before scaling

Compare at least two multilingual encoders and a simple same-language lexical baseline. Initial candidates are [multilingual-e5-large](https://huggingface.co/intfloat/multilingual-e5-large) and [BGE-M3](https://huggingface.co/BAAI/bge-m3). These are candidates, not a claim that either is best for biblical language. Follow each model's input recipe; E5 specifies the same `query:` prefix for both sides of symmetric similarity tasks and has a 512-token limit.

Modern multilingual support does not establish competence in Biblical Hebrew/Aramaic or Koine Greek. Specialized [Ancient Greek sentence-embedding research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.13116) offers a later comparison point. [COMET](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.213/) demonstrates an evaluation approach trained against human translation judgments; its general results do not establish suitability for this corpus.

Construct a proposed 200-passage benchmark spanning narrative, poetry, law, and argument, with short/long verses and both ordinary and difficult examples. Separate a representative sample from a challenge set. Include:

1. Meaning-preserving paraphrases and changes to archaic wording.
2. Controlled changes to negation, speaker, names, quantities, or participant roles; keep synthetic examples outside the published scripture corpus.
3. Genuine translation differences, including legitimate ambiguity and differences in source readings.
4. Missing/merged/misaligned passages and footnote contamination.
5. Same-topic but non-equivalent verses, which test more than simple verse retrieval.

Use blind human comparison where possible, preserving disagreement and ambiguous cases. For fidelity judgments, use readers competent in the relevant source language. An LLM can help propose examples, but it cannot be the sole judge of its own evaluation set.

Measure human-ranking agreement, useful findings among the top-ranked differences, sensitivity to controlled meaning changes, and false alarms on paraphrases. Break results down by genre, length, language, and model. Bootstrap at passage/book grouping level where appropriate; avoid treating overlapping windows as independent evidence. Reserve held-out books or passages for final evaluation and document likely Bible exposure in model training.

Proposed pilot gates, to freeze before evaluating held-out data:

- Every published comparison has a traceable alignment; unresolved units receive no semantic score.
- Controlled meaning changes outrank their paired meaning-preserving edits in at least 80% of challenge comparisons.
- At least 70% of the top 30 flagged natural differences are independently judged useful for close reading, with reviewer disagreement reported.
- Semantic ranking adds value beyond the lexical baseline on held-out examples; report effect size and uncertainty rather than choosing the most flattering model run.

These are initial engineering targets, not established scientific cutoffs. If the experiment fails, keep the useful text-comparison interface and revise the metric before promoting semantic rankings. Cross-language and source-conditioned features require their own evaluations.

## 6. Architecture and scale

Use an offline Python pipeline for acquisition, parsing, alignment, embeddings, scoring, and evaluation. Keep manifests and small fixtures in Git; store immutable raw snapshots and generated arrays outside normal source history. Cache by content hash plus model/preprocessing revision.

For the first web client, use TypeScript and React with a chart library supporting zoom, linked selection, and accessible interaction. Start with versioned, compressed static data partitioned by pair and book. A backend can follow when user uploads, annotations, or expensive dynamic queries require it. No vector database is necessary for known aligned-passage comparisons.

Pipeline: source snapshots → normalized segments → reviewed alignment → versioned embeddings → per-pair scores and summaries → chart and linked reader.

Planning estimate: 31,000 units × 3 editions × 1,024 dimensions × 4 bytes is approximately 381 MB of raw float32 embeddings per model, before metadata. Three pairwise float32 score arrays are only about 0.37 MB. Keep vectors offline and deliver scores/text to the browser. These are rounded capacity estimates, not measurements of an ingested corpus.

Pair counts grow as T(T−1)/2. Precompute all pairs for the small initial catalog; at larger scale, compute requested pairs on the server and cache them. Book summaries must aggregate aligned passage comparisons and report coverage; whole-book embedding similarity would answer a different question and can hide local differences.

Before a full embedding run, measure tokens, throughput, memory, and projected compute cost on the pilot. No provider, paid service, hosting platform, or spending commitment is selected in this plan.

## 7. Delivery sequence

| Milestone | Deliverable | Completion condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Corpus and alignment spike | Pinned WEB/BSB/ASV snapshots, source manifests, John pilot, cross-genre benchmark candidates | Reproducible parsing; every pilot unit mapped or explicitly unresolved; known edge cases represented |
| 2. Measurement experiment | Two model runs, lexical baseline, blind-review rubric, held-out evaluation report | Signal meets agreed gates or limitations drive a revised metric |
| 3. Interactive vertical slice | John chart, linked scatterplot and reader, selectors, reference navigation, ranked list, shareable URL | A reader can find a difference, inspect context, and reopen the exact selection; keyboard and narrow-screen flows verified |
| 4. Corpus expansion | Shared full-corpus coverage, book/chapter overviews, download/export and provenance details | Coverage and aggregate denominators visible; loading remains responsive on a representative device |
| 5. Research expansion | One additional modern language; then source-edition and latent-reference experiments | Each new comparison mode validated independently before public interpretation claims |

For the interface, target selection updates within 100 ms once the book is loaded; measure on a declared reference device and dataset. Test mapping edge cases, cache invalidation, deterministic scores within declared numerical tolerance, and chart-to-reader correspondence. Do not require network model calls for ordinary navigation.

The immediate next implementation task is milestone 1 plus a small measurement run. Its purpose is to establish that the data aligns and that the proposed score surfaces interesting differences before investing in the full visualization.

## 8. Decisions still open

- Confirm English comparisons as the first priority versus modern cross-language or source-language comparisons.
- Select the intended audience: curious readers initially, with enough provenance for research-oriented use; scholarly fidelity claims would require a deeper review process.
- Confirm the initial editions and coverage, including any must-have licensed translations or broader canon requirements.
- Decide how to obtain independent human judgments for the benchmark.
- Select hosting and a final embedding model after pilot measurements.

All research links were inspected on October 14, 2026. Downloads, source snapshots, alignment correctness, model performance, and UI behavior remain to be verified during implementation.
